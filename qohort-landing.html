Who it's for

Two groups keep asking us for the same thing from opposite ends.

Students and recent graduates

College teaches programming, databases and algorithms well. What it rarely gives you is time in the untidy part of software work: vague requirements, a deploy that fails at 11pm, data that doesn't look like the sample, a reviewer who asks why you did it that way.

AI tools have made writing code cheap. The engineers who stand out are the ones who can tell whether generated code is right, fit it into a larger system, and own it when it breaks.

Developers with 2–4 years' experience

You already ship software. What you're missing is AI-specific: document pipelines, retrieval, agents, evaluation, and the cost and reliability problems that come with them.

You shouldn't have to sit through a React module to get there, so you don't. The entry assessment places you where the new material starts.

How a tier moves

  1. Weeks 1–2BaselineWe check where you are, introduce the system you'll build and set expectations.
  2. Weeks 3–7BuildYou implement the core pieces through guided modules and lab reviews.
  3. Weeks 8–9Break itWe add load, bad data, security problems and failures. You work out what went wrong.
  4. Weeks 10–11HardenYou fix what broke, write down your decisions and go through code and architecture review.
  5. Week 12DefendYou present the system and answer technical questions on your own.

You pass by showing the work.

Not by attending. Every assessment puts you in front of people who will ask hard questions.

  • Build tasks. Implement a feature or subsystem under stated constraints.
  • Code review. Explain your choices, spot the risks, respond to feedback.
  • Debugging. Fix a broken system you've never seen before.
  • Architecture review. Compare options and argue for one.
  • Evaluation. Design test cases, read the failures, report the limits honestly.
  • Technical defence. Explain the whole system on your own, including the parts an AI tool helped write.

On AI coding tools

Use them. Engineers use them every day and so will you. You must understand what the tool produced, check that it works, disclose where it did significant work, and be ready to defend every line.

Assessments include unaided code-reading and debugging. If you can't explain it, it doesn't count.

The standard we hold every project to

Why is it built this way? How do we know it works? What happens when it fails? What would you change next?

Questions

Do I have to start at Tier 1?

No. Each tier can be entered directly after passing its entry assessment.

How long is the programme?

Each tier is 12 weeks. The full four-tier pathway is 48 weeks. Because modules are self-paced, some people move faster through material they already know.

When can I join?

At the start of any month. Take the readiness assessment and we'll place you in the right tier.

I have a full-time job. Can I still do this?

Yes. The evening and weekend track runs the same curriculum and assessments, with labs outside working hours.

Is it online or in person?

Our initial catchment is Pune, PCMC, Moshi and Alandi. Confirm the format for your intake on the application page.

What happens in Tier 4?

You work on a real problem in a domain such as legal technology, alongside domain experts and under supervision. Which domains are open depends on the partner projects active when you reach Tier 4.

Can I use AI coding assistants?

Yes, with disclosure. You remain responsible for your tests and changes, and assessments include unaided code-reading and debugging.

Do you guarantee a job or placement?

No. We provide supervised practice, assessed evidence of your capability and structured job-search support. Hiring decisions remain with employers.

What does the credential mean?

A Qollabra competency credential records the tier, assessment date and evidence of what you demonstrated. It is not a university degree or government-accredited diploma.
